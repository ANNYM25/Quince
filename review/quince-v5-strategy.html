1 · The answer first

Quince is discovered on social but not yet trusted

Governing thought: Quince pays rented platforms to re-buy customers it already has, and starves the owned channels where customers actually come back. The first order buys trust. Quince never asks for the relationship while that trust is fresh.

One sentence: Move $9M of the fixed $120M from rented channels that re-buy existing customers into owned channels that earn the second purchase. That lifts 90-day repeat from 24% to about 31% and cuts blended CAC 12 to 14%, and no money moves until Adobe CJA proves it.

90-day repeat~31%From 24%. Range 29.5–33%. Target ≥ 30%.
Blended CAC (Exhibit J measure)−12 to −14%$62 → ~$53–55. Target ≤ −10%. Strict new-customer CAC ≈ −8%.

✓ No budget increase

Every move is a reallocation inside the $120M. Net change $0, released in gated tranches.

✓ No price-led retention

No discounts, credits or points. Perks are access only (early access, restock-first). Proof replaces price.

✓ Grounded in Adobe CJA

Every finding cites a panel (A0 to A7, plus Attribution IQ and the Cohort Table). Every budget move waits for a CJA holdout.

Sequence: Option B funds it (months 0–3) → Option A builds it (months 3–9) → Option C proves it (from day 1, trusted from month 6). Three levers: Capture every buyer, Win the Golden Month, Never pay twice. One thread through all three: trust.

The target arithmetic

Repeat rate = (engaged share × engaged repeat) + (non-engaged share × non-engaged repeat) CJA today: 0.245 × 28.1% + 0.755 × 4.7% = 6.9 + 3.5 = 10.4% (A2 shows 10.5% on its ~3-month window) Lever logic: raise the engaged share (Lever 1), raise repeat inside each segment (Lever 2), buy customers who look like repeaters (Lever 3 seeding). Strict CAC = $120M ÷ new customers = $120M ÷ ~1.94M = $62 (1.94M = Σ channel spend ÷ channel CAC, Exhibit D and G) Falls only if new customers rise at the same spend: cart recovery and product-page proof. Effective CAC (Exhibit J) = same spend over more purchasing customers as repeat rises +6 pts repeat → −10% to −12%, so each point ≈ −1.67% to −2.0%

CJA repeat is a ~3-month figure on a 60,000-person illustrative sample, so we use CJA for relative effects and the case (24%) for the baseline.

3 · Behavioural analysis · the centrepiece

Why first buyers don't come back, stage by stage

Each stage pairs CJA evidence with a named behavioural mechanism, a non-price intervention inside one of the three levers, and the CJA test that proves or kills it.

MEASURED the behaviour is visible in CJAHYPOTHESIS the mechanism is inferred; the test decides
Stage 1 · Discovery on socialHYPOTHESIS

Price-quality heuristic and ambiguity aversion

CJA evidenceProspecting brings 45.5% of buyers from 27,691 people (A3b and channel panel). Visitor-to-buyer: prospecting 4.26%, organic 5.08%. MechanismPeople read price as a signal of quality. Grade-A cashmere at half the usual price, from a brand with no stores, sounds too good to be true, so the visitor discounts the claim. Unknown quality is avoided more than known risk. InterventionCreator wear-tests and material proof in prospecting creative (Lever 3, creators funded from search). Show the evidence, never a lower price. CJA testSplit prospecting ad sets: proof creative vs current. Read product-page reach and cart rate by campaign in a Fallout panel.
Stage 2 · Product pageMEASURED

Perceived risk at the moment of commitment

CJA evidence60,000 → 36,566 view a product → 11,339 add to cart. 25,227 people leave here, the largest absolute loss in the funnel (A1). MechanismOnline-only apparel carries fit and quality risk the buyer can't check by touch. Without signals (reviews in use, care, the return promise), the safe choice is to leave. Social proof lowers that risk. InterventionProduct-page proof block: fabric close-ups, care guide, return and exchange promise above the fold, creator wear-tests (Lever 1). CJA testA/B on the product page, randomized by person. KPI: product view → cart. Estimate: +1 pt (31% → 32%) EST.
Stage 3 · CartMEASURED

Unfinished tasks fade without a reminder

CJA evidence11,339 add to cart → 2,592 order: 77.1% never buy, above the ~70% benchmark (A1, Exhibit C). A0 logs 3,642 email and 1,215 SMS sends, yet 1 message follows 11,610 cart adds (A4). MechanismZeigarnik effect: an unfinished task stays salient for a short window and then fades. Goal gradient: people speed up when they see they are close to done. Endowment: an item already "in my cart" feels partly owned. InterventionTriggered cart reminders within hours, framed as "your cart is saved" with the item image. No discount in the message (Lever 1). CJA test10% randomized holdout on cart flows. KPI: cart → order. Read the Flow from add_to_cart for the share now followed by a message.
Stage 4 · CheckoutMEASURED

Friction and the default at the last step

CJA evidence5,818 start checkout, 2,592 finish: 55.4% drop (A1). App converts carts at 27.1% vs web 22.7%. MechanismDefault effect: people keep the option already chosen for them. Most checkouts have already captured an email, so the abandoner is reachable. InterventionOrder tracking opens in the app by default, with SMS as the fallback. Marketing consent is a separate, unticked box. Checkout-abandon recovery to the typed email (Lever 1). CJA testKPI: app installs and opt-ins per 100 orders, checkout → order. Segment: checkout-initiated, no order, email captured.
Stage 5 · First deliveryHYPOTHESIS

Peak-end: trust is highest when the parcel opens

CJA evidenceOnly 24.5% of buyers engage after order one (A2), though email touched 53.6% (A3b). Texts drew 30.5% clicks per send, email 2.0% (A0). MechanismPeak-end rule: people judge an experience by its best moment and its end. The unboxing is when the quality claim is confirmed. Endowment: registering an item makes it feel more owned. InterventionA card in every box: "Register your piece" for its care guide, exchange window and restock-first alerts. It is the only owned touch that reaches 100% of buyers (Lever 1). CJA testCard QR carries a campaign code. KPI: registrations per 100 boxes, engaged share. Estimate: 5% of non-engaged buyers register EST.
Stage 6 · Days 0–30MEASURED

Habits form early or not at all

CJA evidenceCohort Table, weekly: engaged buyers return at 5.6% in week 2 against 2.1% for all buyers and about 1.0% for non-engaged buyers (derived). Weeks 1–4 hold half of engaged buyers' 11-week returns (16.9% of 34.7%), and the engaged curve peaks in week 2, then decays. For all buyers the weekly rate stays flat at 1–2%: no habit forms. App after order one: 21.5% repeat (57/265), SMS 11.7%, non-engaged 4.7% (A5, A2). MechanismHabit formation needs a cue, a routine and a reward, repeated early. Implementation intentions ("when it arrives, I'll set up care reminders") turn intent into action. Reciprocity: a useful service before any ask. InterventionDays 0–30 journey: care and fit tips, quick-exchange, then access-only perks (early access, restock-first). Help first, sell second (Lever 2). CJA testRebuild the same Cohort Table on the pilot and holdout segments. Success: the pilot's weeks 1–4 return rate moves toward the engaged curve (16.9% cumulative) from the all-buyer curve (6.4%). KPI: 30-day repeat vs holdout (Gate 1).
Stage 7 · Days 30–90MEASURED

"Buy less, buy better" blocks a second sweater

CJA evidenceCashmere 13.7% repeat on 1,054 buyers. Home 20.8%, jewelry 19.5%, beauty 18.5% (A6). Retargeted visitors convert at 4.85%, below organic's 5.08%. MechanismMental accounting: "my sweater budget is spent." Identity: a quality-first shopper won't buy the same item twice. But a new category sits in a different mental account. InterventionCross-sell by material and season: a winter cashmere buyer is shown a cashmere throw or linen sheets in spring, and jewelry and beauty as gifts (Lever 2). Stop paying retargeting to re-show the same sweater (Lever 3). CJA testSequential segment: first order cashmere, then a later order in Home, jewelry or beauty. KPI: second-category rate within 60 days.

Nudges we will not use

  • Fake scarcity or countdown timers. They break trust, the very asset this plan builds.
  • Pre-ticked marketing consent. Not valid consent for marketing texts under TCPA, and it erodes trust.
  • Discount drips ("10% off if you come back"). They break constraint 2 and train buyers to wait.
  • Confirm-shaming and hidden unsubscribe. Short-term clicks, long-term opt-outs.

Rented reach keeps getting costlier; owned reach doesn't

Exhibit: one stacked bar of the $120M: rented $94M (78%) vs owned $6M (5%).

  • Number first: open with the figure, then the reason.
  • 20 seconds, then stop.
  • Concede, then bridge: "Partly, and we measured it…" then back to the gate.
  • Never guess a number; say what panel would answer it.

Posture

  • Calm certainty on measured numbers; open about hypotheses.
  • No speaker talks over another.
  • The team agrees the three numbers everyone can quote: 28.1 vs 4.7, 77.1, 23.0 vs 23.4.
Finals Q&A · the 15 hardest questions

Number first, under 20 seconds

1. Isn't the app effect selection bias?"Partly, and we measured it. Counting only app use after the first order cuts repeat from 25.9% to 21.5%, still 4.6 times non-engaged buyers. That's why repeat is a range and the pilot has a randomized holdout."
2. Can a 60,000-person sandbox size a $120M decision?"No, and we don't ask it to. CJA gives relative effects; the case gives the scale: 4.2M customers, $62 CAC. Every dollar waits for a live holdout."
3. How does higher repeat lower CAC?"On Exhibit J's measure, the same spend produces more purchasing customers: −12 to −14%. On strict CAC, cart recovery and product-page proof add new customers at the same spend: about −8%."
4. Your base case only just clears 30%."Base is 31%, downside 29.5%, and buffers take the downside to 30%. Gate 2 checks for 28% at month 9, with time to add buffers."
5. Half your buyers got an email. How are only a quarter engaged?"Exactly the point. 53.6% received email, 24.5% engaged. Texts got 30.5% clicks per send, email 2.0%. Reach isn't the gap; relevance is."
6. How do you know trust is the problem?"We treat it as a hypothesis. 25,227 of 36,566 product viewers leave without a cart add. A person-level product-page test decides it, read in CJA."
7. Isn't a member perk a discount?"No. Early access and restock-first alerts are access, not price. No credits, points or partner discounts."
8. Isn't a pop-up a distraction?"It's a $1M capture test inside the existing brand line, one metro, one season. Entry is through the app, so CJA follows every attendee. If their repeat doesn't beat matched buyers, it stops."
9. Why move money to influencers?"Search brings 14.1% of buyers for 18.3% of budget and repeats at 9%. Influencer brings 13.5% for 13.3%. $1.5M moves only after the branded-search holdout."
10. You don't cut the biggest line."Prospecting brings 45.5% of buyers. We change who it buys by seeding half the ad sets on repeat buyers, inside the same $58M, with a +5% CAC guardrail."
11. Your Attribution IQ shows retargeting at about 12.5% under every model. Isn't it pulling its weight?"Attribution splits credit; it can't tell you whether the sale would have happened anyway. Retargeting's share barely moves across models, so the panel neither convicts nor clears it. Its buyers repeat like organic (23.0% vs 23.4%), and its visitors convert below organic (4.85% vs 5.08%). So tranche 1 only suppresses buyers we can already message, and a holdout decides anything more. The gate works both ways."
11b. What did Attribution IQ actually change in your plan?"Two things. Owned channels take part in 15% of revenue but get under 5% on last touch, so the case for funding them got stronger. Search gains credit under first touch, so we kept its cut behind the geo holdout instead of making it automatic."
12. Won't more messages drive unsubscribes?"We send fewer, triggered messages under one frequency cap, and unsubscribe rate is a guardrail that pauses the next tranche."
13. Why the South?"Largest region, 28.6% of CJA buyers, lowest opt-in, so the most headroom. The holdout is randomized inside the South, and about 2,900 buyers per arm fills in about three weeks."
14. What's the margin story?"LTV:CAC is 4.6x on revenue but about 2.1x on a 45% margin, and a web-only prospecting buyer returns 0.94x. Moving one into the app is worth about $115 of margin."
15. Why is email's repeat 100% in your data?"A tracking seam: owned-channel visits are logged only at purchase. We never quote it, and fixing that tracking is an amber item."

The call's misreads

"$14K per customer, they should be bankrupt"

The sandbox is a 60,000-person illustrative sample. Use $62 and the share-of-spend index.

"Email buyers repeat 100%"

A tracking seam: 74 clicks, 74 orders, 74 repeaters.

"People don't order from the app"

21.5% of app users repeat, 4.6× the baseline.

"Jewelry and beauty are weak"

19.5% and 18.5% repeat, above cashmere's 13.7%.

"The landing drop is the biggest"

More people leave at product → cart (25,227 vs 23,434).

"Paris", "seasonality is proven", "cookie deprecation"

Quince is ~90% US. Three months can't separate season from growth. Chrome kept third-party cookies.
