The upgrade · same three levers, one signature idea each

More creativity, more business sense, more CJA

The behavioural thread your call found and V3 dropped: a customer buying half-price cashmere online from a brand with no stores is running a trust test. The first order buys the trust. The second order spends it. Trust peaks on the day the parcel arrives, so that is when to ask for the relationship. Every lever is timed to that moment.
Lever 1 · Capture every buyer

The box is a channel

75.5% of buyers are unreachable after order one (A2). But every one of them receives a parcel. Quince ships roughly 8 million orders a year ($950M ÷ $118 AOV), so the box is the only owned channel that reaches 100% of buyers at almost no cost.

WhatA card in every first order: "Register your piece." Scanning opens the app (or an SMS thread) with the item's care guide, its exchange window and restock-first alerts for that material. Registration creates the stitched identity CJA needs. Why it worksPeak moment: trust is highest when the product is in hand. Endowment: people engage more with something they have "registered" as theirs. It also answers your team's pop-up instinct with no capex: every parcel is a pop-up store already inside the customer's home. PlusSell-out waitlists. Viral items sell out, so "text me when it's back" turns spike traffic into reachable people before their first order. Then a two-step consent: delivery texts are transactional, and the marketing opt-in is asked inside that thread ("Reply YES for restock alerts"), which keeps you clean on consent rules. CJA proofQR and waitlist entries carry a campaign code. KPI: registrations per 100 boxes, and the share of registered buyers in the reachable segment. SizingIf 5% of otherwise-unreachable buyers register: 3.8% of buyers × ~10 pts haircut-adjusted lift ≈ +0.4 pts repeat.
Lever 2 · Win the Golden Month

Cross the aisle through the material and the season

People buy Quince for materials (Mongolian cashmere, Turkish cotton, European linen), not for departments. And a winter cashmere buyer's 90-day window lands in spring, when nobody buys knitwear. That is part of why cashmere repeats at 13.7%. You spotted the March pattern on the call.

WhatNext-best-product rules by material and season. A cashmere sweater bought in January leads to a cashmere throw or linen sheets at day 21, not a second sweater. Home is the category that keeps people (20.8% in CJA, 33% in the case), and a material bridge is the most natural way into it. PlusGift-date reminders. Jewelry repeats on gifting (27%, Exhibit F), so "save a date" in the app sends curated picks 14 days before. It is non-price and owned, and it repeats every year. CJA proofA sequential segment: first order cashmere, then any order in Home. Cashmere orders by month, read with care: Jan to Mar also contains growth (805, 861, 1,048 orders). SizingNo extra points claimed. This makes your +1.9 pt cross-sell lever believable instead of generic.
Lever 3 · Never pay twice

…and pay once, for the right customer

This closes root cause 1 and is the only upgrade that touches the $58M. Prospecting customers repeat at 18% against 35% for organic (Exhibit D). Today the algorithms are trained on "anyone who bought".

WhatExport the CJA segment of repeat buyers as the lookalike seed and conversion signal for Meta and TikTok prospecting. Run it as a split inside the same $58M: half the ad sets seeded on repeat buyers, half on all buyers. Trade-offBetter customers can cost a little more to win. 1. Can a 60,000-person sandbox size a $120M decision?"No, and we don't ask it to. CJA gives us relative effects, 28.1% vs 4.7%. The case gives the scale: 4.2M customers, $62 CAC. Every dollar waits for a live holdout."
2. Is "no message after an abandoned cart" real, or does your data just not record sends?"It's real. The data logs 3,642 email sends and 1,215 texts, and just one of them follows any of the 11,610 add-to-carts. Quince emails people after they buy, never when they hesitate."
2b. Half your buyers got an email. How are only 24.5% reachable?"Fair, and precise: 53.6% received an email, 24.5% engaged with any owned channel. That's the point. Batch email reaches people and doesn't move them, while texts get five times the clicks from a third of the sends. So it's reach and relevance."
3. Your base case only just hits 30%."Before the two levers we added, yes. With registration in the box and repeat-seeded prospecting it's about 31%, the downside is 29.5%, and Gate 2 at month 9 checks for 28% while there's time to add buffers."
4. You don't touch the biggest budget line."We change who it buys, not how much it spends. Prospecting customers repeat at 18% against 35% for organic. We seed half the ad sets on CJA repeat buyers, inside the same $58M, with a CAC guardrail of +5%."
5. Why the South?"It's our largest region, 28.6% of CJA buyers, with the lowest opt-in. That's the most headroom. The holdout is randomized inside the South so regional differences don't bias the read. At about 2,900 buyers per arm, it's powered within weeks."
6. 39% leave before seeing a product. Why not fix that?"Landing to product view is 61%, in line with the case funnel for social traffic. The gap to benchmark is at the cart: 77% abandonment against 70%. That's where both targets are won."
7. Wouldn't pop-up stores build trust better?"They'd need capex outside the $120M and they don't reach the buyer after order one. The box does. Every parcel is a pop-up store already inside the customer's home."
8. Does a delivery text give you marketing consent?"No, transactional isn't marketing consent. We ask for marketing opt-in inside the delivery thread, at the moment of highest trust, and track that conversion as its own KPI."
9. What's the margin story? LTV:CAC is already 4.6x."That's on revenue. On a 45% contribution margin it's about 2.1x, and a web-only prospecting buyer is 0.94x: we lose money on them. Moving one to the app is worth $115 of margin."
10. What if Attribution IQ shows retargeting is incremental after all?"Then tranche one stops. The gate works both ways. We're committing to the method, not to the cut."
11. How do you stop channel owners gaming this?"One shared CJA workspace, a weekly incrementality council, and scorecards on incremental revenue per dollar instead of platform-reported ROAS."
12. Why is email's repeat 100% in your data?"It's a tracking artifact: owned-channel visits only appear at purchase in this data view. We never quote it, and fixing that tracking is on our amber list."
From the 9 Oct call · do not say these

Traps to avoid in front of judges

"$14K per customer, they should be bankrupt"

On the call, annual spend was divided by sandbox orders. The CJA data is 60,000 people at illustrative scale over about 3 months, not Quince's customer base, so the division is meaningless. Use the case's $62 CAC and channel CACs from Exhibit D.

"Search gives the same as influencer for $22M vs $16M"

The same mistake: CJA counts set against annual budgets. Compare shares with shares instead (see the share-of-spend table), or use Exhibit D: search $58 vs influencer $52 CAC.

"It's seasonality"

January to April also contains growth (805 → 861 → 1,048 orders). Say "consistent with seasonality" and use it to time the next-best product, not as a finding.

"Cookie deprecation"

V3 already caught this one: Chrome kept third-party cookies. Say ATT, platform automation and TikTok ownership.

The case is fictional · how this review handles it

Real brand, invented numbers

Case numbers are the facts

The brief says every Quince figure and the whole CJA dataset are invented. Judges score internal consistency and correct reading, not realism. So the review checks your maths against the exhibits and the sandbox, never against "what Quince really does".

The answer is planted

A synthetic dataset is built "consistent with the exhibits", so the designers chose the signals. V3 found all five: cart abandonment above benchmark, owned and app cohorts retaining better, retargeting looking non-incremental, cashmere repeating least, and Home holding customers.

Artifacts are not insights

Synthetic data has seams: email at 100%, the "No value" row, a partial April. Naming them before a judge does earns credit. Building a finding on them loses it.

Real world only where labelled

External facts (ATT, Everlane, the lawsuit, sentiment) are real and verified, and are labelled as public data. Never present a case figure as a Quince disclosure: the brief forbids it. Creative ideas use things true of the real brand (it ships parcels, sells by material, sells out).
