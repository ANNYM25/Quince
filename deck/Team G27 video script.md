# Team G27 · 5-minute video script (50% on the slide, 50% beyond it)

Each slide has two halves. **On the slide** walks the judge through what they can see. **Beyond the slide** adds the insight the slide can't show by itself. The halves are roughly equal in length.

Target: ~620 spoken words, about 4:45 at ~130 words per minute, leaving room for hand-offs. Every number in the "On the slide" halves is on the slide being shown.

---

## Slide 1 · Cover · Speaker A · ~15 s

**On the slide:** Hi, we're Team G27. Our plan lifts Quince's 90-day repeat from 24% to about 31%, inside the same $120 million, with no discounts.

**Beyond the slide:** Most brands answer rising acquisition costs by spending more or cutting price. We do neither. We own the second purchase.

## Slide 2 · Executive summary · Speaker A · ~50 s

**On the slide:** CAC is up 29% and repeat trails peers. Three levers fix it. Capture every buyer with the Quince Passport, because the quarter who engage make up two-thirds of repeaters. Win the golden month with Material Journeys. Never pay twice: CJA suppression stops us re-buying our own customers. Result: about 31% repeat, CAC down 12 to 14%, $9 million re-aimed at net zero.

**Beyond the slide:** Why a blend? Each option covers another's weakness. Paid precision is fast but builds no relationship. Owned lifecycle builds one but is capped by today's list. Attribution proves both. So retargeting starts it, owned channels build it, and CJA holdouts decide every dollar. That's why no line moves until the data earns it, and why the plan meets all three constraints.

## Slide 3 · Market · Speaker B · ~40 s

**On the slide:** Renting reach is getting dearer and blinder. Only 35 to 50% of iOS users allow tracking, Meta's price per ad is up 12%, and sentiment sits near record lows. Prospecting takes 48% of the budget at $74 a customer and keeps only 18%. App buyers repeat 38% against 13% for web-only, yet owned channels get 5%.

**Beyond the slide:** So every extra dollar in prospecting buys the customer least likely to come back. That's a capital-allocation problem, and price can't solve it in this economy. The fix is to move money toward customers who compound: on the case's own numbers, an app buyer is worth $410 over three years, a web-only buyer $155.

## Slide 4 · Customer (CJA) · Speaker C · ~55 s

**On the slide:** Adobe CJA shows where value leaks. 77% of carts stall, and only one of about 5,500 abandoned paths gets an email. Engaged buyers repeat at 28% against under 5%. Repeat orders peak in week two, and cashmere, our entry product, repeats least.

**Beyond the slide:** Three behaviours sit underneath. An unfinished purchase stays on the mind only briefly, the Zeigarnik effect, so a reminder within hours wins it back. Premium cashmere at half price can feel too good to be true, so proof, not discounts, closes that gap. And we checked selection bias: app users who join after order one still repeat at 21.5%.

## Slide 5 · Competition · Speaker D · ~40 s

**On the slide:** A rival copies a 20% discount overnight and a journey in a quarter, but factory-direct cost plus opted-in buyers takes years. Amazon's ads grow 26% a quarter. Everlane had mature email and still sold. Williams-Sonoma sued over "dupes".

**Beyond the slide:** The lesson is that neither half works alone. Owned channels without a cost edge failed Everlane, and a cost edge without owned reach leaves Quince bidding against Amazon. Together they're defensible, and if rivals escalate, suppression keeps us out of the auction.

## Slide 6 · Capabilities · Speaker E · ~35 s

**On the slide:** The hard part exists: CJA stitches one person across every channel. Shipping events feed that identity, and Journey Optimizer sends the message. Three gaps cost $4.8 million, funded inside the owned line.

**Beyond the slide:** The real constraint is people and process, not technology. And timing matters: the unboxing is the emotional peak, the peak-end rule, so that's when we ask for the app.

## Slide 7 · Next steps · Speaker D · ~50 s

**On the slide:** A 90-day South pilot with a 10% holdout. Each lever has a pass mark: engaged share doubles, 30-day repeat up two points, orders per retargeting dollar beat the holdout. Then $4 million moves from retargeting at month three, search and prospecting only if proven.

**Beyond the slide:** We chose the South because it's the hardest test: fastest new-customer growth, lowest opt-in. If it works there, it scales. And the holdout takes politics out of the budget. In one sentence: Quince stops renting customers and starts owning the second purchase. Thank you.

## Delivery notes

- **Timing:** rehearse with a stopwatch and aim for 4:45–4:50. Every second over 5:00 costs a point.
- **If you run long, cut in this order:** the selection-bias sentence on slide 4, then the escalation clause on slide 5.
- **Hand-offs:** one short phrase each, e.g. "C will show what CJA found." They count toward the time.
- **Speaker order:** E, on slide 6, has the shortest slot; give it to your most nervous speaker (the guide's advice for the second-to-last slide). D closes, so D should be your strongest speaker.
- **Layout:** keep video feeds off the slides, and watch the recording back before submitting.
