# Team G27 · 5-minute video script

Target: ~600 spoken words, about 4:35 at ~130 words per minute, leaving ~20 s for hand-offs. Every number below is on the slide being shown.

## Slide 1 · Cover · Speaker A · ~15 s

Hi, we're Team G27. Quince has mastered the first purchase. Our plan is to own the second one: lifting 90-day repeat from 24% to about 31%, inside the same $120 million, with no discounts.

## Slide 2 · Executive summary · Speaker A · ~50 s

Viral moments built a huge base of first buyers, but CAC is up 29% and repeat trails peers. We blend all three options through three levers.

One: capture every buyer. A Quince Passport card in every box, plus SMS and app opt-in, because the quarter of buyers who engage make up two-thirds of repeaters.

Two: win the golden month. Material Journeys turns cashmere buyers into home buyers by day 21.

Three: never pay twice. CJA suppression lists stop us re-buying customers we already own.

The result: repeat to about 31%, blended CAC down 12 to 14%, and $9 million re-aimed at net zero. No new budget, no discounts, every dollar proven in Adobe CJA.

## Slide 3 · Market · Speaker B · ~40 s

Why now? Renting reach is getting dearer and blinder. Only 35 to 50% of iOS users allow tracking, Meta's price per ad is up 12%, and consumer sentiment sits near record lows. So price can't be the lever.

Look at the table: prospecting takes 48% of the budget, costs $74 a customer and keeps only 18%. Owned customers do the opposite. App buyers repeat 38% against 13% for web-only, and they're worth $410 against $155. Yet owned channels get just 5% of the budget. That's a capital-allocation problem.

## Slide 4 · Customer (CJA) · Speaker C · ~55 s

Adobe CJA shows where value leaks.

First, the cart: 77% of carts stall, and only one in about 5,500 abandoned paths gets a follow-up email. An unfinished purchase stays on the mind for a short window, the Zeigarnik effect, so a reminder within hours wins it back.

Second, reachability: buyers who engage with email, SMS or the app repeat at 28% against under 5%. And when we control for timing, app users still repeat at 21.5%.

Third, timing: repeat orders peak in week two, and cashmere, the product that brings people in, repeats least.

So the data tells us who, when and what: reach every buyer, act in month one, move them to a second category.

## Slide 5 · Competition · Speaker D · ~40 s

Can rivals copy this? Partly. A 20% discount is copied overnight. Journeys and suppression lists take a quarter or two. But factory-direct cost combined with opted-in buyers takes years.

Three rivals prove the point. Amazon's ad business grows 26% a quarter, so a paid-reach auction is one Quince can't win. Everlane ran mature email and SMS and still sold to Shein, so owned channels alone aren't a moat. And Williams-Sonoma sued over "dupes", so trust must come from product proof, not comparison ads.

Our move is straight up the map: keep the price, own the relationship.

## Slide 6 · Capabilities · Speaker E · ~30 s

Can Quince execute? The hard part already exists: CJA stitches one person across web, app, email and SMS. Shipping events flow into that identity, and Journey Optimizer triggers the message.

Three gaps remain, costing $4.8 million and all funded inside a growing owned line: activation at $1.5 million, a lifecycle team and creative at $2.8 million, and a growth-analytics council at half a million.

## Slide 7 · Next steps · Speaker D · ~45 s

We prove it before we scale it. A 90-day South pilot with a 10% randomized holdout, and every lever has a pass mark: the engaged share doubles, 30-day repeat is up two points, and orders per retargeting dollar beat the holdout while new customers hold.

Then the money moves only where it's earned: $4 million from retargeting at month three, search at months four to six, and prospecting only if it's shown to be over-credited.

The holdout decides, not the loudest channel owner.

One sentence: Quince stops renting customers and starts owning the second purchase. Thank you. Our CJA evidence is in the appendix.

## Delivery notes

- **Timing:** rehearse with a stopwatch and aim for 4:45–4:50. Every second over 5:00 costs a point.
- **Cutting:** if you run long, cut the Zeigarnik sentence on slide 4 first and the Williams-Sonoma line on slide 5 second.
- **Hand-offs:** one short phrase each, e.g. "C will show what CJA found." They count toward time.
- **Speaker order:** E, on slide 6, is the shortest slot; give it to your most nervous speaker (the guide's advice for the second-to-last slide). D closes, so D should be your strongest speaker.
- **Layout:** keep video feeds off the slides and watch the recording back before submitting.
